You're Done With Squarespace. Here's How to Get Your Claude Site Actually Live.

You already have a website. You're just done paying $36/month to fight a template. Claude Code builds exactly what you want — and getting it live is three steps. Here's what they actually are.

Melanie Dunn | AI & Automation | 7 min read

If you've been on Squarespace, you know the ceiling. The template does what the template does. You want a custom layout and suddenly you need a developer, or you need to upgrade to Business, or the thing just isn't possible without injecting code into a box that wasn't designed for it.

Claude Code removes that ceiling entirely. You describe what you want — the layout, the colors, the copy, the vibe — and it builds it. You can make changes in plain English on a Wednesday afternoon and they're live by the time you close your laptop.

The part nobody explains clearly: once Claude builds your site, it lives on your computer as a folder of files. Getting it to an actual URL involves three steps. They're not hard. They just have a lot of unfamiliar terminology around them, which makes them sound harder than they are.

Here's what's actually happening — and how to get your Squarespace domain pointing at your new Claude site.

GitHub holds your files on the internet — not as a website, but as a storage place that Cloudflare Pages watches. When you push changes to GitHub, Cloudflare sees them and updates your live site automatically. That's the whole mechanism behind the editing workflow I use: change something in Claude Code, push it, reload the browser, done.

Step 1: GitHub (free, ~5 minutes)

Create a free account at github.com. Create a new repository — think of it as a named folder for your project. Make it private if you want; files aren't visible to the public just because they're on GitHub. Cloudflare is what makes them a website.

You don't need to understand most of what GitHub does. You need three things: the account, the repo, and a way to push your files into it. Claude Code can handle the pushing — just tell it "commit and push everything to my GitHub repo" and give it the URL. Or download GitHub Desktop if you'd rather click than type in a terminal. Both work fine.

Step 2: Cloudflare Pages (~4 minutes of clicking)

Go to cloudflare.com, create a free account, navigate to Workers & Pages. Hit Create → Pages → Connect to Git. Pick your GitHub repo.

Important

For a plain HTML site — no React, no build step, just files — leave the build settings completely blank. No build command. No output directory. Just hit Deploy.

Cloudflare gives you a free URL ending in .pages.dev that works immediately. Share it, people can see your site. That's a real live URL. Connect your own domain whenever you're ready, but the .pages.dev URL is a perfectly reasonable place to start.

Step 3: The editing workflow (ongoing)

This is the part I wanted most when I was figuring it out. Here's what day-to-day actually looks like:

  1. Open your site folder in Claude Code
  2. Tell it what to change — it edits the files
  3. Tell it "commit and push"
  4. Cloudflare picks up the update, usually within a minute
  5. Refresh your live URL — it's there

No logging into a CMS. No clicking around a visual editor. Your files are on your computer, Claude can edit them, and the live site stays in sync. I've made changes to my site mid-meeting because it took less time than writing a task for it.

Set up CLAUDE.md — your brand brief Claude reads automatically

Here's the thing about switching from Squarespace: you already have a brand. You know your colors, your fonts, your tone. The problem with building in Claude is that every new session, it forgets. You end up re-explaining yourself constantly.

Fix this immediately with a file called CLAUDE.md in your site folder. Claude reads it automatically every time you open the project. Think of it as the brief you'd hand a new designer — except you only write it once.

What to put in it: your business name and what the site is for. Your exact colors (hex codes — pull them from your Squarespace brand settings before you leave). Your fonts. Your voice: the tone, who you're talking to, what to avoid. Any standing rules: "always link to my contact page," "never use stock photo language," "keep copy short."

If you've already told Claude something three times in three different sessions, that belongs in CLAUDE.md. Write it once, never say it again.

Skills: for when you're ready to go deeper

Claude Code has a skill system — reusable instruction sets you install once and invoke by name, like plugins. You don't need them on day one. But if you're building something with a real visual identity and more than a few pages, they start to matter.

The ones that shaped this site: a brand skill holding my full visual identity (colors, fonts, aesthetic rules), a voice skill keeping my writing sounding like me, and a frontend design skill guiding how Claude structures HTML and CSS. Skills live in your ~/.claude/ folder and you call them with a slash command.

Build first. Get it live. Add skills when Claude needs reminders about things you've already told it three times.

Netlify vs. Cloudflare Pages

I started on Netlify. I switched to Cloudflare. Here's the honest comparison:

Netlify

Easier forms

  • Built-in form handling — just add netlify to your form tag
  • Submissions email you automatically, no third-party accounts
  • Free tier covers personal sites easily
  • Slightly slower global performance
  • DNS separate if domain is elsewhere
Cloudflare Pages

Better performance

  • Massive global CDN — noticeably faster
  • Tighter DNS integration if domain is at Cloudflare
  • Edge functions available if you want to get fancier later
  • No built-in forms — need Web3Forms or similar
  • Slightly more setup for forms

If you want the easiest possible setup and don't care about performance differences: Netlify. If you want DNS all in one place and a faster site: Cloudflare. Both are free. Either works.

Getting your Squarespace domain to point at your new site

You get a free .pages.dev URL the moment you deploy — that's already a real, shareable link. But you almost certainly want your existing domain pointing there, not a Squarespace site you're paying for.

Good news: you don't have to transfer your domain. You just redirect it. Here's exactly what I did — my domain is registered at Squarespace, same as yours probably is. I went into Squarespace's domain settings, found the nameserver section, and changed the nameservers to Cloudflare's (Cloudflare gives you the exact values when you add your domain). That tells the internet: "for anything involving this domain, ask Cloudflare now." Then in Cloudflare, I added one CNAME record pointing at my Pages site. Within a few hours, my domain resolved to the new site.

At that point you can cancel your Squarespace subscription. The domain stays registered at Squarespace; it just lives somewhere new.

The permanent fix

The setup above works well — Squarespace holds the domain registration, Cloudflare handles DNS. The cleaner long-term move is transferring the domain registration itself to Cloudflare Registrar, which puts everything in one place and usually costs a few dollars less per year (Cloudflare charges at-cost, no markup). There's a 60-day transfer lock to be aware of, so no rush — but it's worth doing eventually.

Contact forms and newsletter signups

On Netlify: Add netlify to your <form> tag. Done. Submissions email you automatically. Zero extra accounts required.

On Cloudflare Pages: You need a form service. I use Web3Forms — free for up to 250 submissions/month. Get an access key from their site, add it as a hidden input field, set your form's action to their API endpoint. Submissions land in whatever email you tell it. Under 15 minutes to set up.

Newsletter: MailerLite is free up to 1,000 subscribers and 12,000 emails/month. They give you a copy-paste embed snippet. Drop it into your HTML. No backend, no server code. I have mine on the Melsplains index page — setup including matching the site design took about 20 minutes total.
